# Waste Management — City / Community

## Overview
Municipal solid waste (MSW) management is one of the core responsibilities of a city's civic authority. It covers the collection, transportation, processing, recycling, and disposal of waste generated by households, commercial establishments, and public spaces. Poor waste management leads to public health risks, soil and water pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions from decomposing organic waste in landfills.

---

## Household Waste Segregation

### Why Segregation at Source Is Critical
When waste is mixed together, recyclable and compostable material is contaminated and ends up in landfill. Source segregation — sorting waste at home before it is collected — is the foundation of an effective waste management system.

### Standard Segregation in Indian Cities (as per Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016)
- **Green bin / wet waste** — Organic/biodegradable waste: food scraps, vegetable and fruit peels, garden waste, leftover cooked food.
- **Blue bin / dry waste** — Recyclable waste: clean paper, cardboard, plastic bottles and containers, glass, metal cans, tetra packs.
- **Red bin / hazardous domestic waste** — Items requiring special handling: used batteries, medicines, fluorescent bulbs, chemicals, paint containers, syringes.
- **Black bin / sanitary waste** — Soiled diapers, sanitary napkins, bandages — must be wrapped before disposal.

### Tips for Correct Segregation
- Rinse food residue from containers before placing in dry waste.
- Do not wrap wet waste in plastic bags — use newspaper or biodegradable bags.
- Check your city's specific segregation guidelines, as colour codes can vary by municipality.

## Municipal Waste Collection

### Collection Schedules
Most urban local bodies (ULBs) operate door-to-door waste collection, typically daily or on alternate days. Collection vehicles (often compactors or handcarts) follow fixed routes and timings.

### If Collection Is Missed
1. Note the date, time, and location (street name, ward number, landmark).
2. Contact your ward office or the municipal solid waste management helpline.
3. Many cities have a civic app or grievance portal where missed collections can be reported.
4. If the problem is persistent, escalate to the municipal commissioner's office.

### Bulk Waste and Special Collection
- Large items (furniture, appliances, mattresses) require a separate bulk waste collection request.
- Contact your ward office or municipal helpline to schedule a bulk pick-up.
- Do not leave large items on public footpaths indefinitely.

## Illegal Dumping and Fly-Tipping

### Health and Environmental Impact
Illegal dumping of waste in open plots, roadsides, riverbeds, and vacant land creates disease vectors (mosquitoes, rodents), pollutes groundwater, blocks drainage, and is a fire hazard.

### Reporting Illegal Dumping
1. Note the exact location (street, landmark, GPS coordinates if possible).
2. Photograph the dump site if it is safe to do so.
3. Report to the municipal corporation or ward office via phone, app, or in person.
4. Many cities have dedicated civic complaint apps (e.g., Swachh Bharat App in India) for reporting waste dumping.

### What Civic Authorities Are Obligated to Do
Under the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 (India), urban local bodies are responsible for clearing illegal dumps and prosecuting violators. Citizens can follow up on complaints and escalate if unresolved.

## Recycling in the City

### Types of Recyclable Materials
- **Paper and cardboard** — newspaper, magazines, cardboard boxes (dry and unsoiled).
- **Plastics** — PET bottles (Category 1), HDPE containers (Category 2), rigid plastics. Soft plastic films are more difficult to recycle.
- **Metals** — aluminium cans, tin cans, scrap metal.
- **Glass** — bottles and jars (remove lids).
- **Tetra packs / multilayer packaging** — some facilities accept these; check local recycling acceptance.

### How to Recycle Effectively
- Clean and dry recyclables before putting them in the bin or dropping them off.
- Do not wishful-recycle — items that are not accepted contaminate the entire batch.
- Use local scrap dealers (kabaadiwalas) for metals, paper, and electronics — this is an important part of the informal recycling economy.

### E-Waste (Electronic Waste)
- E-waste contains toxic materials and must not be disposed of in regular bins.
- Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations require electronic manufacturers to provide take-back schemes.
- Contact your local municipality or the manufacturer for authorised e-waste collection points.
- Many cities and NGOs organise periodic e-waste collection drives.

## Composting and Organic Waste

### Home Composting
Home composting converts kitchen and garden waste into nutrient-rich compost for plants.
- Use a sealed compost bin or pot with drainage holes.
- Add a mix of wet (fruit/vegetable scraps, food waste) and dry (dry leaves, cardboard) material.
- Avoid adding meat, dairy, or oily foods — these attract pests.
- Turn the compost every few days to aerate it and speed decomposition.
- Compost is ready in 6–8 weeks and can be used for home gardens or donated to community gardens.

### Bulk Organic Waste
- Large-scale organic waste from markets, restaurants, and events should be directed to city composting facilities.
- Many cities have decentralised composting units in residential welfare association (RWA) areas.

## Plastics and Single-Use Plastic Ban

### Banned Single-Use Plastics in India (effective July 2022)
The Government of India banned the manufacture, import, sale, and use of identified single-use plastic items under the Environment Protection Act, including:
- Plastic earbuds, flags, candy sticks, ice-cream sticks
- Polystyrene (thermocol) for decoration
- Plastic plates, cups, glasses, cutlery (forks, spoons, knives, straws)
- Wrapping or packing films around sweet boxes
- Invitation cards with plastic, and cigarette packets with plastic

### What Citizens Can Do
- Carry reusable bags, bottles, and containers.
- Refuse single-use plastic straws and cutlery.
- Buy products with minimal or recyclable packaging.
- Report vendors continuing to use banned plastics to the municipal authority.

## Community Actions for Better Waste Management

- Participate in or organise neighbourhood clean-up drives.
- Join or support a Resident Welfare Association (RWA) that manages decentralised composting and segregation.
- Advocate for better waste collection frequency and infrastructure in underserved areas.
- Support the livelihoods of waste pickers and the informal recycling sector by segregating waste at source.

---

## Key Contacts (City / Community)
- **Municipal Corporation / Ward Office** — for missed collections, illegal dumps, bin overflow, and general waste complaints.
- **State Pollution Control Board** — for large-scale illegal dumping affecting land or water.
- **Swachh Bharat App / Municipal Grievance Portal** — for filing and tracking civic waste complaints online.
- **Local NGOs and Resident Welfare Associations** — for community-led composting, clean-up drives, and awareness.
