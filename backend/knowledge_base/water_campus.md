# Water Management — Campus

## Overview
Water management on campus covers conservation, leak reporting, drinking water quality, and responsible usage across university buildings, dormitories, laboratories, and outdoor spaces.

---

## Common Campus Water Issues

### Leaking Pipes and Fixtures
Leaking pipes, taps, and fixtures are among the most common water waste sources on campus.
- A dripping tap can waste more than 5,000 litres of water per year.
- A continuously running toilet can waste up to 200 litres per day.
- Visible pipe leaks in corridors, basements, or utility rooms should be reported immediately to Campus Facilities or the Maintenance Help Desk.
- Do not attempt to repair plumbing yourself — always report it through the official channel.

### How to Report a Water Leak on Campus
1. Locate the nearest emergency shut-off valve if the leak is severe.
2. Contact the Campus Facilities or Maintenance team via the campus helpdesk number or online portal.
3. Note the exact building, floor, and room number.
4. If water is pooling near electrical fittings, evacuate the area and notify security.

### Low Water Pressure
Low water pressure in taps or showers is often caused by:
- Partially closed supply valves
- Blocked aerators or showerheads (mineral buildup)
- A leak elsewhere in the building reducing pressure
Report low pressure to the Campus Maintenance team for diagnosis.

## Drinking Water Quality on Campus

### Water Safety Standards
Drinking water on campus is typically sourced from the municipal supply and should comply with the national drinking water quality standards set by the relevant authority (e.g., BIS IS 10500 in India, or EPA standards in the US).

### Signs of Drinking Water Problems
- Discolouration (brown, yellow, or cloudy water) — may indicate rust, sediment, or bacterial growth.
- Unusual smell (chlorine, sulphur/rotten egg) — may indicate pipe corrosion or bacterial contamination.
- Unusual taste — may indicate chemical contamination or old pipework.

### What to Do if Drinking Water Seems Unsafe
1. Stop using that water source immediately.
2. Report the issue to Campus Facilities with the location and description of the problem.
3. Do not consume the water until it has been tested and cleared.
4. The facilities team should test the water and flush or replace affected pipes as needed.

## Water Conservation on Campus

### Individual Actions
- Turn off taps while soaping hands or brushing teeth — saves up to 6 litres per minute.
- Report any running or dripping taps you notice on campus.
- Use water-efficient washing machines in campus laundries (full loads only).
- Avoid using drinking water for non-drinking purposes.

### Facility-Level Measures
- Install dual-flush toilets and low-flow aerators on taps.
- Use rainwater harvesting systems for garden irrigation.
- Install leak detection sensors in high-usage buildings.
- Conduct regular audits of water consumption by building.

### Landscaping and Outdoor Water Use
- Water grounds and gardens in the early morning or late evening to minimise evaporation.
- Use drought-resistant native plants to reduce irrigation needs.
- Drip irrigation systems are significantly more efficient than overhead sprinklers.

## Flooding and Waterlogging on Campus

### Causes
- Blocked or inadequate storm drains
- Heavy rainfall exceeding drain capacity
- Low-lying areas with poor gradient

### Immediate Actions
1. Avoid walking through floodwater — it may be contaminated or conceal hazards.
2. Report waterlogged areas to Campus Facilities immediately.
3. Do not park vehicles in flood-prone zones during heavy rain warnings.

### Long-Term Remediation
- Campus management should maintain and regularly clear storm drains.
- Permeable paving in parking areas helps reduce surface runoff.
- Green infrastructure (rain gardens, bioswales) can manage stormwater sustainably.
