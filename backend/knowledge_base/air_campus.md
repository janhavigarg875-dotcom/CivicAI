# Air Quality Management — Campus

## Overview
Air quality on campus is affected by indoor ventilation systems, laboratory fumes, vehicle emissions from campus roads and car parks, dust from construction, and the general urban air environment. Campus administration and facilities teams are responsible for maintaining safe indoor and outdoor air quality.

---

## Indoor Air Quality

### Common Indoor Air Quality Issues on Campus
- **Poor HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning) performance** — inadequate fresh air supply, dirty filters, or broken units cause stale, stuffy indoor air.
- **Mould and dampness** — mould releases spores that worsen respiratory conditions; caused by water ingress, poor ventilation, or condensation.
- **Chemical fumes in laboratories** — solvents, reagents, and gases can pose serious health risks if ventilation hoods are not functioning.
- **Dust and particulate matter** — from construction, carpets, or HVAC systems with clogged filters.
- **Carbon dioxide buildup** — in poorly ventilated classrooms with high occupancy, CO₂ levels can rise above 1,000 ppm, causing drowsiness and reduced concentration.

### Signs of Poor Indoor Air Quality
- Persistent musty or chemical smell
- Frequent headaches, eye irritation, or throat discomfort among occupants
- Visible mould or condensation on walls and ceilings
- HVAC units that are audible but not effectively cooling or ventilating
- Fogging or condensation on windows in enclosed spaces

### What to Do About Poor Indoor Air Quality
1. Report HVAC problems, ventilation failures, or mould to Campus Facilities immediately.
2. In laboratories, always work under a fume hood when handling volatile chemicals; report non-functional hoods before using them.
3. Open windows where possible to increase natural ventilation.
4. Do not block HVAC vents with furniture or equipment.
5. Avoid using aerosol products, strong adhesives, or cleaning chemicals in poorly ventilated spaces.

## HVAC Systems on Campus

### Maintenance Requirements
- HVAC filters should be inspected and replaced on a regular schedule (typically every 3–6 months depending on usage).
- Duct cleaning prevents accumulation of dust, allergens, and microbial growth.
- CO₂ sensors in classrooms and offices can trigger automatic fresh-air ventilation.

### Reporting HVAC Issues
- Report non-functioning air conditioning, heating, or ventilation to Campus Facilities.
- Provide the building name, room number, and a description of the problem (e.g., no airflow, unusual smell, noise).
- In cases of suspected gas leak or chemical smell, evacuate the area and contact Campus Security immediately.

## Vehicle Emissions on Campus

### Sources
- Vehicles idling in campus drop-off/pick-up zones
- Diesel generators during power outages
- Heavy vehicles during construction or deliveries
- Two-wheelers and older vehicles with poor emission controls

### Reducing Vehicle Emissions on Campus
- Encourage cycling and walking through dedicated infrastructure (cycle lanes, secure parking).
- Implement and enforce a no-idling policy in campus drop-off zones.
- Transition campus fleet vehicles (buses, maintenance vehicles) to CNG, electric, or hybrid options.
- Schedule heavy deliveries and construction during low-occupancy hours.
- Promote shuttle services and carpooling to reduce the number of private vehicles on campus.

### Reporting Excessive Vehicle Emissions on Campus
- Report persistent vehicle pollution (e.g., trucks idling for long periods, smoke-emitting vehicles) to Campus Security or the Transport Office.
- Document the vehicle registration number, location, and time if possible.

## Construction Dust on Campus

### Health Impacts of Construction Dust
Fine particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10) from construction sites can penetrate deep into the lungs and worsen asthma, allergies, and respiratory conditions.

### Control Measures
- Construction zones should be screened with dust barriers.
- Water spraying on dry surfaces and debris reduces airborne dust.
- Workers and nearby occupants should wear appropriate masks (N95 or equivalent) when near active construction.
- Report excessive dust from campus construction to the Campus Environmental Officer or Facilities team.

## Outdoor Air Quality Monitoring

### Air Quality Index (AQI)
The AQI measures the overall air quality based on concentrations of key pollutants: PM2.5, PM10, nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), ozone (O₃), carbon monoxide (CO), and sulphur dioxide (SO₂).

| AQI Range | Category | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–50 | Good | No restrictions |
| 51–100 | Satisfactory | Sensitive groups limit prolonged outdoor exposure |
| 101–200 | Moderate | General public reduce prolonged outdoor exertion |
| 201–300 | Poor | Avoid outdoor activity; wear masks outdoors |
| 301–400 | Very Poor | Remain indoors; use air purifiers |
| 401–500 | Severe | Health emergency; avoid all outdoor activity |

Campus administrations should monitor local AQI and issue advisories when the AQI exceeds the Moderate range.

## Key Contacts (Campus)
- **Campus Facilities / Maintenance** — HVAC, ventilation, and building air quality issues.
- **Campus Laboratory Safety Officer** — fume hood failures, chemical exposure incidents.
- **Campus Environmental Officer** — air quality monitoring, green transport initiatives.
- **Campus Security** — gas leaks, emergency evacuations.
