# Air Quality Management — City / Community

## Overview
Urban air pollution is one of the leading environmental health risks worldwide. City air quality is affected by vehicular traffic, industrial emissions, construction activity, biomass burning, and weather conditions. Citizens can protect themselves, reduce their personal contribution, and report violations to civic and regulatory authorities.

---

## Understanding Air Quality

### Air Quality Index (AQI)
The AQI is a standardised scale that communicates how clean or polluted the air is on a given day. It is based on concentrations of key pollutants.

| AQI Range | Category | Health Implications |
|---|---|---|
| 0–50 | Good | Air quality is satisfactory; little or no risk |
| 51–100 | Satisfactory | Minor discomfort for sensitive individuals |
| 101–200 | Moderate | Breathing discomfort for people with asthma, elderly, children |
| 201–300 | Poor | Breathing discomfort for most people on prolonged exposure |
| 301–400 | Very Poor | Serious health impacts on prolonged exposure |
| 401–500 | Severe | Health emergency; affects healthy people too |

Real-time AQI data for Indian cities is available at sameer.gov.in (CPCB). Many cities worldwide have their own monitoring portals.

### Key Pollutants
- **PM2.5** — Fine particles (diameter < 2.5 µm); penetrate deep into the lungs. Major sources: vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, cooking fires.
- **PM10** — Coarser particles (< 10 µm); from dust, construction, road traffic.
- **NO₂ (Nitrogen Dioxide)** — Primarily from vehicle exhaust and power plants; irritates the respiratory tract.
- **O₃ (Ozone)** — Ground-level ozone forms from NOₓ and VOCs in sunlight; worsens in hot weather.
- **SO₂ (Sulphur Dioxide)** — From burning coal and industrial processes; causes acid rain and respiratory problems.
- **CO (Carbon Monoxide)** — From vehicle exhaust and incomplete combustion; dangerous in enclosed spaces.

## Common Sources of Urban Air Pollution

### Vehicular Emissions
Road transport is the dominant source of urban air pollution in most cities.
- Diesel vehicles (trucks, buses, older cars) emit high levels of PM2.5, NOₓ, and black carbon.
- Two-stroke engine vehicles produce high hydrocarbon emissions.
- Traffic congestion increases pollution as idling engines emit more pollutants per kilometre.

### Industrial and Commercial Emissions
- Factories, power plants, and brick kilns emit SO₂, NOₓ, and particulate matter.
- Restaurants and dhabas using solid fuel emit PM2.5 and black carbon.
- Industrial zones are regulated under environmental law; violations can be reported to the Pollution Control Board.

### Construction and Road Dust
- Unpaved roads, construction sites, and demolition activities generate large quantities of PM10.
- Wind erosion of bare soil and stockpiles contributes to ambient dust.
- Effective controls include water spraying, covering stockpiles, and using dust suppressants.

### Open Burning
- Burning of crop residue (stubble burning) is a major seasonal source of PM2.5 in many regions.
- Municipal solid waste burning in open areas is illegal in most cities and should be reported.
- Leaf burning, garbage burning, and tyre burning are prohibited under pollution control laws.

## Protecting Yourself from Air Pollution

### On High-Pollution Days (AQI > 200)
- Limit outdoor activities, especially vigorous exercise.
- Keep windows and doors closed during peak pollution hours (typically morning rush and evening).
- Use an N95 or N99 respirator mask if outdoor activity is unavoidable.
- Use a HEPA air purifier indoors if available.
- Vulnerable groups (children, elderly, pregnant women, people with asthma or heart disease) should remain indoors.

### At Home
- Avoid smoking indoors.
- Ensure adequate ventilation when cooking, especially on gas or solid fuel stoves.
- Use exhaust fans in kitchens and bathrooms.
- Do not burn incense or candles in enclosed spaces for extended periods.

## Reporting Air Pollution Violations in the City

### Who to Contact
- **State Pollution Control Board (SPCB)** — for industrial and commercial air pollution violations (India).
- **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)** — for national-level violations; also manages the Sameer air quality app.
- **Municipal Corporation** — for open waste burning, construction dust, and vehicle emission complaints.
- **Transport Authority** — for smoke-emitting vehicles on roads.

### How to File a Complaint
1. Note the exact location, time, and nature of the pollution.
2. Photograph or video the source if it is safe to do so.
3. Submit to the relevant authority via their online portal, helpline, or the SAMEER app (India).
4. Keep a reference number for follow-up.

### Citizen Rights
Under environmental law, citizens have the right to a clean environment. Regulatory authorities are obligated to investigate complaints about air pollution within specified timeframes. Persistent inaction can be escalated to the National Green Tribunal (NGT) in India or equivalent bodies in other jurisdictions.

## Community Actions to Improve Air Quality

- Support and use public transport to reduce private vehicle trips.
- Walk or cycle for short distances — both reduce emissions and improve personal health.
- Advocate for expansion of green cover (trees and urban forests) in your neighbourhood.
- Participate in tree plantation drives.
- Avoid purchasing products from industries known to violate pollution norms.
- Organise or join community campaigns for clean air awareness.

## Key Contacts (City / Community)
- **State Pollution Control Board (SPCB)** — for industrial and large-scale emissions.
- **Municipal Corporation Environment Cell** — for open burning, construction dust, vehicular emissions.
- **CPCB Sameer App / Portal** — for real-time AQI and complaint submission (India).
- **Local Health Department** — for health advisories and medical guidance during severe pollution events.
